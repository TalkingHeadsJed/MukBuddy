Tile and masonry work produces difficult combinations of material.

A contractor may collect:

- Tile dust
- Concrete dust
- Mortar powder
- Grout dust
- Broken tile
- Sand
- Cement residue
- Water
- Slurry
- Packaging and spacers

Fine mineral dust requires effective source capture and filtration.

Broken tile requires durable hoses and collection containers.

Water and slurry require a correct wet-pickup configuration.

One system must not be expected to handle every material in the same configuration.

---

## Quick Answer

A tile or masonry contractor should prioritize:

- Application-appropriate dust extraction
- Strong maintained airflow
- Fine-particle filtration
- Tool and shroud compatibility
- Automatic or efficient filter cleaning
- Durable hoses
- Separate handling for sharp fragments
- Wet/dry capability where needed
- Controlled slurry disposal
- Easy tank and hose cleaning
- Secure collection bags or liners

For continuous cutting, grinding, or surface preparation, use equipment designed for the tool and application.

## The Tile and Masonry Debris Split™

The work creates three distinct material classes.

```text id="p9v44n"
Fine Dry Dust

↓

Sharp Solid Fragments

↓

Wet Slurry
```

Each class requires a different collection strategy.

---

## Fine Dry Dust

Examples:

- Tile cutting dust
- Grout powder
- Mortar dust
- Concrete dust
- Sanding dust

Prioritize:

- Source capture
- Fine-dust collection
- Appropriate filter
- Secure system seals
- Maintained airflow
- Controlled disposal

---

## Sharp Fragments

Examples:

- Broken tile
- Stone chips
- Metal trim
- Fasteners
- Hardened mortar

Prioritize:

- Large open hose
- Durable tank
- Puncture resistance
- Separate collection from fine-dust bags

Sharp tile fragments can tear disposable and reusable collection systems.

---

## Wet Slurry

Examples:

- Wet cutting residue
- Concrete slurry
- Grout water
- Mortar cleanup water

Prioritize:

- Correct wet configuration
- Large hose
- Float protection
- Controlled drainage
- Immediate cleaning
- Appropriate disposal

Slurry should not be allowed to harden inside the vacuum.

## The Material Separation Principle™

```text id="8aqio6"
Fine Dust Collected Separately

+

Sharp Debris Collected Separately

+

Slurry Collected Separately

=

Lower Clogging and Equipment Damage
```

Separating materials reduces bag tears, paste formation, and difficult disposal.

## Feature 1: Tool and Shroud Compatibility

Dust capture begins at the tool.

Check compatibility with:

- Tile saw enclosure
- Grinder shroud
- Mixing station
- Drill attachment
- Surface-preparation tool
- Mortar-removal system

A strong vacuum cannot capture dust that escapes outside the shroud.

---

## Feature 2: Maintained Airflow

Fine mineral dust can load filters quickly.

Look for:

- Large filter area
- Automatic filter cleaning
- Suitable dust bag
- Pre-separation
- Secure seals
- Easy airflow monitoring

---

## Feature 3: Durable Hose

A tile contractor’s hose may encounter:

- Abrasive particles
- Sharp fragments
- Slurry
- Repeated dragging

Choose:

- Reinforced walls
- Durable cuffs
- Appropriate diameter
- Easy cleaning
- Replaceable components

---

## Feature 4: Controlled Dry-Dust Disposal

Suitable options may include:

- Fine-dust fleece bag
- Approved reusable system
- Plastic liner in a compatible extractor
- Multi-stage collection

Avoid open dumping of concentrated mineral dust.

---

## Feature 5: Slurry Management

Wet slurry may:

- Settle
- Separate
- Harden
- Block the drain
- Coat the float
- Abrade components

Clean the vacuum promptly after use.

---

## The Slurry Hardening Curve™

```text id="h5zuh4"

Material Bonds to Surfaces
```

Immediate cleaning reduces equipment damage.

## Dry and Wet Equipment Strategy

Contractors performing frequent dry and wet work may benefit from separate machines.

### Dry Extractor

For:

- Grinding
- Cutting
- Fine powder
- Source capture

### Wet/Dry Cleanup Vacuum

For:

- Slurry
- Water
- Tile fragments
- Final cleanup

This prevents moisture from contaminating fine-dust filters and bags.

## Common Mistakes

### Mixing Dry Powder and Water Inside the Vacuum

This can create hardened cementitious residue.

### Collecting Tile Shards Into Fine-Dust Bags

The bag may tear.

### Using a Basic Cleanup Vacuum for Continuous Grinding

Airflow may collapse rapidly.

### Ignoring the Shroud

Poor source capture allows dust to spread.

### Leaving Slurry in the Tank

It may harden around the drain and float.

### Treating a Cyclone as Final Filtration

Fine particles still require appropriate filters.

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### Can a shop vacuum collect tile dust?

It can collect limited dust when configured correctly, but continuous cutting or grinding may require an application-specific extractor.

#### Can it collect tile fragments?

Yes, provided the hose and tank can handle sharp debris. Avoid delicate bags.

#### Can a wet/dry vacuum collect grout water?

It may collect compatible liquid when configured for wet pickup, but disposal and cleaning must be handled correctly.

#### Why does mortar dust clog filters quickly?

The fine particles remain suspended and penetrate filter pleats.

#### Should dry and wet cleanup use separate vacuums?

Separate machines can reduce contamination and changeover labor for frequent professional use.

#### Are reusable bags suitable?

They may help with compatible dry dust but should be protected from sharp fragments and moisture.

## Key Takeaways

Tile and masonry cleanup involves three different problems:

- Fine dry dust
- Sharp debris
- Wet slurry

The strongest system separates these materials and uses the correct configuration for each.

**Do not ask one bag, one filter, and one hose setup to handle powder, broken tile, and slurry equally well.**
