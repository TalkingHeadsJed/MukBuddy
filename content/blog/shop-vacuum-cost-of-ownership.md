Ask someone how much their shop vacuum cost...

And they'll probably tell you exactly what they paid at the store.

"$199."

"$350."

"$600."

But that's only the beginning.

Imagine buying a work truck and calculating only the purchase price.

Ignoring:

- Fuel
- Oil changes
- Tires
- Insurance
- Repairs
- Maintenance

Nobody would do that.

Yet contractors do it with shop vacuums every day.

The purchase price is often one of the **smallest** expenses over the life of the machine.

The real cost comes from everything that happens after you bring it to the jobsite.

---

## Quick Answer

The true cost of owning a shop vacuum includes far more than the purchase price. Disposable bags, replacement filters, downtime, labor, maintenance, reduced productivity, and motor wear often cost significantly more over the life of the vacuum than the vacuum itself.

Understanding Total Cost of Ownership (TCO) helps contractors make better purchasing decisions and reduce long-term operating expenses.

---

## The Purchase Price Is Only the Admission Ticket

Buying the vacuum gets you into the game.

Operating it is where the real expenses begin.

Every day your vacuum works, it consumes resources.

Some are obvious.

Others quietly drain your profits without you noticing.

Professional contractors don't evaluate equipment by purchase price.

They evaluate it by **cost per year** and **cost per job**.

---

### 🔬 Vacuum Science

Airflow affects economics.

That may sound strange...

But it's true.

When airflow decreases:

- Cleanup takes longer.
- Motors run hotter.
- Filters load faster.
- Operators stop more often.
- Productivity falls.

Small airflow losses eventually become financial losses.

## Cost #1 — Replacement Bags

Disposable bags seem inexpensive.

One bag isn't much.

Neither are two.

Or ten.

But over months and years, recurring purchases add up.

High-volume contractors may go through hundreds of bags annually.

The purchase price becomes a subscription.

---

## Cost #2 — Filters

Filters are consumable components.

Every filter has a lifespan.

Fine dust shortens that lifespan.

The more drywall, concrete, and sanding dust you collect...

The more frequently filters require cleaning—or replacement.

---

## Cost #3 — Labor

Labor is usually the largest hidden cost.

Imagine two contractors.

Both vacuum the same room.

Contractor A finishes in 20 minutes.

Contractor B spends:

- Cleaning filters
- Changing bags
- Fighting reduced airflow
- Making extra passes

He finishes in 35 minutes.

The vacuum didn't just cost more.

The labor did.

---

## Cost #4 — Downtime

Every time the vacuum stops working efficiently...

So does the crew.

Waiting while someone:

- Empties a bag
- Cleans a filter
- Searches for replacement parts
- Troubleshoots airflow problems

Costs real money.

Downtime isn't measured in minutes.

It's measured in payroll.

---

## Cost #5 — Motor Wear

Running with restricted airflow increases operating temperatures.

Higher temperatures accelerate wear.

Eventually motors fail earlier than expected.

Replacing a motor—or replacing the entire vacuum—is significantly more expensive than preventing airflow restriction in the first place.

---

## Cost #6 — Lost Productivity

This may be the most expensive cost of all.

Imagine every cleanup taking:

Five extra minutes.

Now multiply that by:

- Four cleanups a day
- Five days a week
- Fifty weeks a year

That's more than **80 hours** of lost productivity annually.

For one vacuum.

Now imagine an entire fleet.

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### What is Total Cost of Ownership (TCO)?

The combined cost of purchasing, operating, maintaining, and eventually replacing equipment.

---

#### Why do contractors underestimate vacuum costs?

Because many expenses—like downtime and labor—aren't shown on an invoice.

---

#### What's usually the biggest hidden cost?

Lost labor productivity often exceeds the cost of bags, filters, or maintenance.

---

#### Does airflow affect operating costs?

Yes. Better airflow generally means faster cleanup, lower motor temperatures, and less maintenance.

---

#### Should I buy the cheapest vacuum?

The lowest purchase price doesn't always produce the lowest lifetime cost.
