Some movement inside a vacuum collection bag is normal.

The vacuum creates lower pressure inside the tank, causing flexible materials to pull inward.

But a bag that folds over the inlet, pulls completely flat, tears, or prevents debris from entering indicates a problem.

Common causes include:

- Incorrect bag installation
- Wrong bag size
- Incompatible bag design
- Blocked bag surface
- Fine-dust loading
- Overfilled bag
- Moisture
- Inlet obstruction
- Plastic liner collapse
- Restricted filter or exhaust airflow

The correct fix depends on whether the vacuum uses a porous collection bag or a nonporous plastic liner.

---

## Quick Answer

If a shop vacuum bag collapses:

1. Turn off and unplug the vacuum.
2. Confirm the bag is compatible with the vacuum.
3. Check that the collar is fully seated.
4. Position the bag away from the inlet.
5. Inspect for overfilling or fine-dust loading.
6. Check the hose, filter, and exhaust for restriction.
7. Replace wet, torn, or permanently restricted bags.
8. Do not substitute an ordinary trash bag for an approved vacuum liner.
9. Follow the manufacturer’s instructions for plastic liners.
10. Test airflow before returning to work.

A porous collection bag and a plastic liner behave differently and should not be diagnosed the same way.

---

## The Pressure Equalization Principle™

A porous vacuum bag allows air to pass through its material.

A plastic liner does not.

```text
Porous Collection Bag

Air Passes Through Material

↓

Pressure Can Equalize

Plastic Liner

Air Cannot Pass Through Material

↓

Pressure Difference Pulls Liner Inward
```

This is why an ordinary plastic trash bag may collapse immediately inside a vacuum tank.

---

## Normal Bag Movement vs. Failure

### Normal

- Bag expands around available tank space
- Material shifts slightly during startup
- Bag remains attached to inlet
- Debris enters normally
- Suction remains stable

### Abnormal

- Bag covers the inlet
- Bag pulls completely flat
- Collar comes loose
- Bag tears
- Suction drops suddenly
- Dust bypasses into tank
- Filter loads rapidly

---

## Cause 1: Incorrect Installation

The bag may have been:

- Twisted
- Folded over the inlet
- Installed backward
- Trapped under the lid
- Positioned against the filter cage
- Attached only partially to the inlet

Remove and reinstall it according to the vacuum and bag instructions.

---

## Cause 2: Wrong Bag Size

A bag that is too large may:

- Fold over itself
- Cover the inlet
- Become trapped under the lid
- Interfere with the float

A bag that is too small may:

- Stretch
- Pull off the collar
- Tear
- Restrict usable capacity

Tank gallon rating alone does not guarantee compatibility.

---

## Cause 3: Incompatible Collar

The collar must fit the inlet securely.

Problems include:

- Opening too large
- Opening too small
- Weak cardboard collar
- Missing retaining feature
- Incorrect inlet shape
- Collar damaged during reuse

A loose collar allows dust to bypass the bag.

---

## The Collar Load Principle™

The collar carries both airflow pressure and collected weight.

```text
Airflow Pull

+

Bag Weight

+

Movement

=

Collar Stress
```

A weak or poorly fitted collar may separate even when the bag material remains intact.

---

## Cause 4: Fine-Dust Loading

Drywall, concrete, and sanding dust may block the bag’s pores.

The bag can appear only partially full while airflow through the material has already collapsed.

Signs include:

- Gradual suction decline
- Bag pulling tightly inward
- Higher motor pitch
- Hotter operation
- Fine dust coating the bag surface

Replace or empty the bag based on airflow—not only visible volume.

---

## Cause 5: Bag Is Overfilled

Collected material may prevent the bag from distributing around the tank.

An overfilled bag can:

- Reach the inlet
- Stress the collar
- Tear during removal
- Restrict airflow
- Press against the filter cage

Stop before material reaches the inlet opening.

---

## Cause 6: Moisture Entered the Bag

Water can weaken or seal paper bag material.

Moisture may come from:

- Damp debris
- Residual water in the tank
- Wet hose
- Condensation
- Accidental liquid pickup

A wet paper or fleece bag should generally be replaced.

---

## Cause 7: Filter Restriction

A clogged filter reduces airflow through the entire vacuum.

This may change pressure inside the tank and cause flexible bag material to pull inward more aggressively.

Inspect:

- Primary filter
- Pre-filter
- Filter-cleaning mechanism
- Exhaust vents

---

## Cause 8: Hose or Attachment Blockage

A blockage before the tank creates low airflow and high pressure difference.

Check:

- Floor nozzle
- Crevice tool
- Hose cuffs
- Reducers
- Internal hose bends

---

## Cause 9: Plastic Liner Collapse

Plastic liners require a vacuum or separator designed to manage pressure around the liner.

An ordinary trash bag may:

- Pull into the inlet
- Block the airflow path
- Tear
- Wrap around internal components

Do not cut improvised vent holes unless the equipment manufacturer specifically authorizes the method.

## Bag Collapse Diagnostic Table

| Symptom | Likely Cause | First Action |
|---|---|---|
| Bag covers inlet | Incorrect positioning | Reinstall bag |
| Bag pulls flat immediately | Incompatible liner or restriction | Confirm bag type |
| Collar comes off | Wrong fit or excess weight | Replace correctly |
| Bag collapses gradually | Fine-dust loading | Replace or empty |
| Bag tears | Sharp debris or overfilling | Separate debris |
| Dust outside bag | Collar or seam failure | Stop and inspect |
| Plastic liner pulls upward | Pressure imbalance | Use approved system |
| Bag becomes soft | Moisture | Replace and dry tank |

## Common Mistakes

### Using an Ordinary Trash Bag

It may collapse because air cannot pass through it.

### Reusing a Damaged Disposable Bag

The collar and seams may no longer seal.

### Filling the Bag to Maximum Volume

Airflow capacity may be reached earlier.

### Ignoring Fine-Dust Loading

The bag may be restrictive while appearing empty.

### Installing the Bag Against the Inlet

Debris cannot enter properly.

### Collecting Wet Debris

Moisture can destroy dry bag material.

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### Is it normal for a vacuum bag to pull inward?

Some movement is normal. Blocking the inlet, tearing, or severe suction loss is not.

#### Why does my plastic liner collapse?

Plastic does not allow pressure to equalize through its material. Use only an approved liner system.

#### Why does the bag collapse when it is not full?

Fine dust may have blocked the bag’s airflow pathways.

#### Can a clogged filter cause bag collapse?

Yes. Restrictions anywhere in the airflow chain can change pressure and reduce performance.

#### Can I tape the bag to the inlet?

Use the attachment method intended by the vacuum or bag manufacturer. Improvised sealing may interfere with operation.

#### Why did the bag tear near the collar?

The bag may be overfilled, incorrectly fitted, wet, or carrying excessive weight.

## Key Takeaways

Bag collapse usually results from:

- Incorrect fit
- Poor positioning
- Fine-dust restriction
- Overfilling
- Moisture
- Pressure imbalance
- Incompatible plastic liners

**A collection bag should capture debris while allowing the vacuum’s airflow path to remain open.**
