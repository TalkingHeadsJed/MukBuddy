## What the disposable costs you

DeWalt DXVA19-4102 bags run about $20.99 for a 3-pack on Amazon, or roughly $7 per bag (prices as of October 2026). That sounds reasonable until you run the numbers on a busy vac:

- Fine-dust crew (drywall, concrete): 2 to 3 bags a week
- Annual cost: roughly $730 to $1,100 per vac
- Over 3 years: $2,200 to $3,300

The vac itself might have cost $150. The bags cost more than the machine over its life, and the bill never stops.

## What the reusable costs you

Muk Buddy is $99 once. No packs. No reorders. No running to Home Depot or Amazon every few weeks.

At two bags a week, it pays for itself in about two months. At three bags a week, faster. After that, the savings continue for as long as you own the vac.

## Why it works better (not just cheaper)

The cost math is obvious. The performance difference is the part most people don't expect.

A DXVA19-4102 is a standard single-path bag. Dust and air go through together. As the bag fills, fine drywall or concrete dust packs against the filter media and chokes airflow. Suction drops. The motor strains. By lunch, the bag is "full" even if it's barely loaded.

Muk Buddy is a 2-chamber design. Debris drops into the first chamber. Airflow passes through the second. The filter stays clean, suction stays steady, and the motor isn't fighting a clog. That's why it holds suction through the job, not just through the first hour.
