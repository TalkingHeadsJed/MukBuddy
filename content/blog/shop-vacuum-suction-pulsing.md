A shop vacuum should normally produce steady airflow.

If suction repeatedly rises and falls, the system is experiencing an intermittent restriction or power problem.

The vacuum may:

- Pull strongly
- Lose suction
- Recover
- Repeat the cycle

Common causes include:

- Moving hose clog
- Float movement
- Foam
- Loaded filter
- Collapsing bag
- Automatic filter cleaning
- Loose electrical connection
- Motor protection
- Blocked attachment

The rhythm of the pulsing can help identify the cause.

---

## Quick Answer

If suction pulses:

1. Turn off and unplug the vacuum.
2. Remove the attachment.
3. Inspect the hose for moving debris.
4. Check the bag.
5. Inspect the filter.
6. Check the float and tank after wet pickup.
7. Look for foam.
8. Determine whether automatic filter cleaning is operating.
9. Inspect the cord, plug, and switch.
10. Stop using the vacuum if power cuts out, burning odor appears, or the motor overheats.

Mechanical airflow pulsing and electrical power cycling require different solutions.

## The Intermittent Restriction Principle™

```text
Air Path Opens

↓

Suction Returns

↓

Debris or Float Moves

↓

Air Path Closes

↓

Suction Falls

↓

Cycle Repeats
```

Anything moving inside the airflow path can create pulsing.

---

## Cause 1: Moving Hose Clog

A piece of debris may move back and forth inside the hose.

Examples include:

- Plastic
- Cloth
- Paper
- Leaves
- Insulation
- Hair clump
- Long wood shaving

Airflow pulls the debris toward a restriction.

The opening closes.

Airflow drops.

The debris falls away.

The cycle restarts.

---

## Cause 2: Attachment Sealing to the Surface

A flat nozzle may seal tightly against:

- Plastic sheet
- Carpet
- Smooth floor
- Fabric
- Flexible liner

The vacuum pulls the tool down.

Airflow stops.

The attachment releases.

Airflow returns.

---

## The Surface-Seal Cycle™

```text
Nozzle Contacts Surface

↓

Opening Seals

↓

Airflow Stops

↓

Pressure Releases

↓

Nozzle Lifts

↓

Airflow Returns
```

Use an attachment designed to maintain an air opening.

---

## Cause 3: Float Movement

During wet pickup, liquid or foam may lift the float intermittently.

The float closes the motor air path.

When suction stops, the float drops.

The vacuum begins pulling again.

This can repeat rapidly.

Turn off the machine and empty the tank.

---

## Cause 4: Foam

Foam is light and unstable.

It may repeatedly reach the float or filter area.

Common sources include:

- Shampoo
- Detergent
- Carpet cleaner
- Floor cleaner
- Grooming solution

The tank may appear only partially full.

---

## Cause 5: Bag Blocking the Inlet

A loose or poorly positioned bag may move across the inlet.

Airflow pulls it into the opening.

Suction drops.

The bag falls away.

Airflow returns.

Reinstall or replace the bag.

---

## Cause 6: Filter Restriction

A heavily loaded filter may contribute to unstable airflow.

Some dust layers shift or release during operation, temporarily changing resistance.

Clean or replace the filter using the approved method.

---

## Cause 7: Automatic Filter Cleaning

Some dust extractors intentionally pulse or reverse airflow to clean the filter.

The user may hear:

- Repeated thumping
- Brief suction changes
- Regular timed pulses

This may be normal operation.

Review the equipment instructions.

---

## Normal Filter-Cleaning Pulse™

```text
Vacuum Operates

↓

Cleaning Mechanism Activates

↓

Brief Airflow Change

↓

Dust Released From Filter

↓

Normal Airflow Returns
```

Regular timing suggests a designed cleaning cycle.

Random pulsing suggests a fault or obstruction.

---

## Cause 8: Collapsing Hose

A soft hose may repeatedly flatten and reopen.

This may occur because of:

- Heat damage
- Thin hose walls
- Severe restriction
- Tight bends
- Incompatible replacement hose

Replace structurally damaged hose.

---

## Cause 9: Thermal Protection

A motor may shut down when overheated and restart after cooling.

Signs include:

- Longer off periods
- Hot housing
- Burning odor
- Declining runtime
- Restart only after waiting

Stop using the vacuum until the cause is identified.

---

## Cause 10: Electrical Connection Problem

Intermittent power may result from:

- Damaged cord
- Loose plug
- Worn switch
- Faulty receptacle
- Extension-cord connection
- Internal wiring problem

Electrical pulsing often changes motor speed completely rather than merely changing hose airflow.

---

## Airflow Pulse vs. Power Pulse

| Symptom | Likely Type |
|---|---|
| Motor sound stays on but suction changes | Airflow restriction |
| Motor repeatedly stops completely | Electrical or thermal |
| Pulsing after water pickup | Float or foam |
| Regular timed thump | Automatic filter cleaning |
| Hose visibly moves | Clog or collapse |
| Nozzle jumps on floor | Surface sealing |
| Burning odor | Overheating or electrical |
| Pulse changes when hose removed | Hose or attachment |

---

## Diagnostic Sequence

### Step 1: Remove Attachment

If pulsing stops, inspect the attachment or surface interaction.

### Step 2: Remove Hose

If pulsing stops, inspect the hose.

### Step 3: Check Tank and Bag

Look for inlet blockage.

### Step 4: Check Filter

Inspect for heavy restriction.

### Step 5: Check Float

Especially after wet use.

### Step 6: Check Power

Stop if the motor is electrically cycling.

## Pulsing Diagnostic Table

| Pattern | Likely Cause | Action |
|---|---|---|
| Rapid pulse with wet pickup | Float or foam | Empty and inspect |
| Random pulse with debris | Moving clog | Clear hose |
| Pulse only on smooth floor | Nozzle sealing | Change attachment |
| Regular timed pulse | Filter cleaning | Confirm normal operation |
| Motor shuts off when hot | Thermal protection | Stop and diagnose |
| Pulse when cord moves | Electrical damage | Unplug immediately |
| Bag visibly moving | Inlet blockage | Reinstall bag |
| Hose flattening | Restriction or weak hose | Clear or replace |

## Common Mistakes

### Continuing After Float Pulsing Begins

Liquid or foam may be approaching the motor area.

### Assuming Every Pulse Is a Motor Failure

Many pulses are caused by airflow blockage.

### Disabling Automatic Filter Cleaning

It may be essential for maintained performance.

### Ignoring Cord-Related Pulsing

This may indicate an electrical hazard.

### Repeatedly Restarting an Overheated Vacuum

Thermal shutdown indicates a problem.

### Using a Flat Tool That Seals Completely

The nozzle needs an air path.

---

## Frequently Asked Questions

#### Why does suction pulse during water pickup?

The float or foam may be intermittently blocking airflow.

#### Why does it pulse while vacuuming plastic?

The attachment may be sealing to the flexible material.

#### Is filter-cleaning pulsing normal?

It may be normal on equipment with automatic filter cleaning.

#### Why does the motor turn fully off and back on?

Thermal protection or an electrical fault may be involved.

#### Can a full bag cause pulsing?

Yes. The bag may block the inlet or create unstable restriction.

#### Why does the hose jump?

A moving clog, pulsing pressure, or collapsing hose may be responsible.

---

## Key Takeaways

Pulsing suction usually comes from one of three categories:

- Moving airflow restriction
- Float or foam interaction
- Electrical or thermal cycling

First determine whether the motor remains powered.

**Steady motor sound with changing suction suggests an airflow problem. Complete power interruption suggests an electrical or thermal problem.**
